Problem 10 · Reflection draft

Reflection

1. How each agent did

  • Ticket 101 — Inventory: Good: checked the exact SKU, size, and quantity and caught the zero-stock issue and replenishment blocker. Limitation: it could only report the shortage; it could not create stock or contact the vendor.
  • Ticket 101 — Customer Service: Good: turned verified facts into a polite, professional draft without promising a restock date. Limitation: it did not resolve the underlying inventory problem.
  • Ticket 102 — Facilities: Good: used desk.date_today and correctly determined the $2,400 rent was upcoming, not overdue. Limitation: it prepared an internal conclusion rather than taking any payment action.
  • Ticket 102 — Accounting: Good: checked the obligation and balance and respected the human-approval rule. Limitation: it did not make the payment, which was correct because no approval existed.
  • Ticket 103 — Inventory: Good: identified 8 available versus 20 requested, making the 12-unit shortage clear. Limitation: it could not solve the shortage itself.
  • Ticket 103 — Accounting: Good: verified the $22 cost and $58 list price and did not invent a discount. Limitation: it had no purchase to approve because the team had no authorization.
  • Ticket 103 — Customer Service: Good: prepared a clear shortage draft based on the verified quantity and pricing facts. Limitation: it could not promise an alternative or delivery date.

2. Actual versus Expected

  • 101: Matched the expected Inventory → Customer Service plan and used the expected inventory tools. No extra specialist was sent.
  • 102: Matched the expected Facilities → Accounting plan. Accounting reviewed payment implications but correctly stopped before payment because human approval was missing.
  • 103: Matched the expected Inventory → Accounting → Customer Service plan. Facilities was not needed because no replenishment or facilities blocker needed investigation.
  • Overall: The actual run was streamlined as planned, and all three tickets resolved with no cash change.

3. What would be simpler as one agent with tools?

  • One agent could read the ticket, call the relevant MCP tool, summarize the result, and update the status with fewer handoffs and less token use.
  • That would be simpler for straightforward tickets like 101 and 102, where the main work is one evidence check plus a short draft or decision.
  • The five-agent design is stronger for separation of duties: Accounting independently checks money, Customer Service owns tone, and the Boss controls delegation and approvals. A single agent could miss a safety check or make an overconfident promise, so human approval and strict tool permissions would still be necessary.

4. Three new problems the team could solve

  • Inventory shortages: Inventory checks stock, and Customer Service communicates verified alternatives to the customer.
  • Facilities issues: Facilities investigates the problem, and Customer Service communicates any customer impact.
  • Payment questions: Accounting investigates the charge, and Customer Service helps address the customer’s concern.

Possible future specialist extensions

  • Returns and refunds: Add a Returns Specialist plus tools for return eligibility and authorized refunds.
  • Supplier performance problems: Add a Procurement Specialist plus tools to track deliveries and prepare vendor-contact drafts.
  • Product defects: Add a Quality Assurance Specialist plus tools to identify complaint patterns, flag affected inventory, and escalate safety concerns.
